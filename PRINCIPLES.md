# Principles

A work unit needs a governing intent: one dominant reason to exist. Many activities
can derive from that intent without becoming separate product decisions.

Decision coherence asks whether important decisions are coupled. Informal “decision
entropy” means unresolved material choices the worker would otherwise invent; it is
not mathematical entropy and is not scored. Reduce ambiguity before reducing scope.

An owner unknown depends on product, business, architecture, policy, compatibility
or authority. Whether sessions survive migration needs an owner decision. An
implementation unknown can be researched or derived by a competent worker: where the
existing serializer lives does not require the owner to navigate the code.

Semantic boundaries follow independently executable or rejectable decisions. Do not
split a cohesive compatibility migration merely because it touches dozens of modules.
Task size is not LOC, file count, component count or an estimate of working hours.

Verification coherence asks whether independent review can deliver one meaningful
verdict. Rejection locality asks whether a failure points to a bounded repair or
sprawls across unrelated decisions. HIGH rework locality means localized repair;
LOW means diffuse repair. Neither is a probability of success.

Task shaping compensates for today's fallible agents. Better implementation and
verification agents may support broader work units. Empirical outcomes and
counterexamples should refine this framework over time. v0.1 makes no claim of
scientific validation and contains no weighted readiness formula.
